Most consulting is sold by the day. It's flexible, it's easy to start, and it puts all the risk on you: if the work
takes longer, you pay more, and the person deciding how long it takes is the one being paid by the day.

For open-ended work that's sometimes the only honest option. For a review, it isn't. So our platform review is a
fixed price, a fixed scope and a fixed two weeks.

## What fixed price changes

**You know the number before you say yes.** From £10,000 plus VAT, agreed up front, and it doesn't move. That makes
it something you can approve, budget for and compare, rather than an estimate with a range.

**The incentive flips.** On a day rate, a slow review earns more. On a fixed price, the only way we come out ahead
is by being good at reviews: knowing where to look, recognising patterns from the last fifty platforms, and getting
to the findings that matter quickly. That's the skill you're paying for.

**Scope has to be clear**, which is good for both sides. The review covers six areas: infrastructure as code,
Kubernetes clusters, delivery pipelines, security and access, cost, and reliability and recovery. We agree on a
short call what's in and what's out, and read-only access to the rest. If something interesting turns up outside
the scope, we'll note it, and you decide what to do about it.

## What you get, exactly

A written report with every finding ranked by impact and effort. A short list of quick wins. A suggested order for
the bigger changes. A walkthrough with your team, and a follow-up call a month later. The full list is on
[the platform review page](/platform-review/), and
[what the findings tend to be](/blog/platform-audit-findings/) is in an earlier post.

## When fixed price is the wrong tool

We don't fix-price everything. Building a platform, migrating a workload or running a delivery team for a quarter
all involve decisions that can't be made before the work starts. For those we work in short, well-defined phases,
each with its own scope and price, so you're never committing to more than the next phase. The platform review is
often phase zero: it produces the list the next phase is planned from.

## What it says about how we work

A fixed price only works if the work is repeatable, the scope is honest and the findings are useful enough that
people come back. We've designed the review to be all three. If it doesn't tell you something you didn't know, and
what to do about it first, it hasn't done its job.

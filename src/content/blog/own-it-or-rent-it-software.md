For almost any process there's a subscription product that does something like it. Scheduling, quoting, stock,
customer records, job management. They're usually good, often cheap to start, and you should use them where they
fit. The question is how to tell when they don't.

## When to rent

Use an off-the-shelf product when:

- **Your process is standard.** If you do this the same way as most businesses like yours, someone has built the
  tool, and they've thought about edge cases you haven't hit yet.
- **It's not where you compete.** Payroll and accounts are important and they aren't what makes customers choose
  you. Rent them.
- **You can live with its way of working.** Every product has opinions. If you can adopt them without losing
  something that matters, that's fine.
- **The per-user price works at your size.** Ten users at £30 a month is £3,600 a year, which is less than almost
  any custom build.

## When to own

Build something when:

- **The process is the thing you're good at.** If customers choose you because of how you handle their job, don't
  let a tool change it.
- **You're fighting the tool.** Spreadsheets alongside it to cover the gaps, "we just put that in the notes field",
  training new staff in the workarounds. You're paying a subscription and still doing it by hand.
- **You need two tools to talk to each other and they won't.** Much of what we build is the connecting piece: the
  job system that raises the invoice, the booking that updates the calendar and texts the customer.
- **The per-user price has stopped making sense.** Fifty users on site at £30 a month is £18,000 a year. A custom
  app for that process often costs less than two years of that, and then it's yours.
- **You're worried about depending on it.** Prices go up. Products get bought, changed or shut down. Getting your
  data out is rarely as easy as getting it in.

## Questions to ask before renting

1. What happens to my data if I leave? Can I export all of it, in a usable form?
2. What's the price at twice my current size?
3. What have they changed or removed in the last two years?
4. Who owns the customisations I pay for?

## Questions to ask before building

1. Will I own the code, and could someone else maintain it?
2. What does it cost to run each month, and who's it paid to?
3. What's the smallest version that's worth having?
4. Who looks after it after it's built?

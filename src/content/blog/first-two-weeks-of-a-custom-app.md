People who've never commissioned software tend to expect one of two things: months of silence followed by a big
reveal, or a sales deck followed by a surprise invoice. Here's what the first two weeks actually look like with us.

## Before anything is built: the process on one page

We spend the first few days understanding how the process works today. Not how the manual says it works. How it
actually works, including the workarounds, the sticky notes and the things only one person knows.

That means a couple of hours with the people who do it, ideally watching them do it. The output is a one-page
description of the process, in your words, with each step, who does it, what they need to know and what goes wrong.
You check it. We don't start building until it's right, because every hour spent here saves a day later.

## What we ask of you

- **A couple of hours from the people who do the work**, early on. Not the owner's opinion of the process; the
  people in it.
- **Examples.** Real (anonymised) quotes, job sheets, bookings. The weird ones are the most useful.
- **A decision-maker** who can say yes or no to small questions within a day. "Should the reminder go out at 24 or
  48 hours?" shouldn't wait a week.
- **Access** to anything the app needs to connect to, such as your accounting package, when the time comes.

That's about it. You don't need to write a specification. You don't need to know what a database is.

## Week one: the skeleton

By the end of the first week there's something to click on. It's rough, it's missing most things, but it's the
real app, running on the real hosting, showing the main screen and the main action. A job can be created. A
booking can be made.

This is deliberate. Seeing a real screen changes what people ask for. Nearly everyone looks at the first version
and says "oh, actually, what we really need is…". Better to hear that in week one than in month three.

## Week two: the working slice

The second week turns the skeleton into one complete path through the process, start to finish. A job is created,
assigned, done on site with a photo and a signature, and appears in the office as finished. It's not everything the
app will do. It's the single most valuable thing it will do, working.

From here, depending on the size of the job, there are usually two to six more weeks of adding the rest, in the
order you've told us matters. You see it every week, and you can start using it before it's finished if you want
to.

## What's different from a bigger software company

Mostly that the people you talk to are the people building it. There's no account manager, no handover to a team
offshore, and no document that translates what you said into something else. The engineers are the same ones who
build cloud platforms for banks, payment providers and logistics companies. What changes for a small business is the
conversation, not the quality.

"How much does an app cost?" is a fair question with a frustrating answer: it depends. This post is an attempt at a
more useful one, with real ranges and what moves you within them.

## Rough ranges

| What it is | Example | Typical range |
|---|---|---|
| One process, a few screens | A job sheet with photos and a sign-off, or a booking form with a calendar | £5,000 to £15,000 |
| One process, connected to things you already use | The above, plus it raises the invoice in your accounting package and sends the customer a text | £15,000 to £40,000 |
| A system several teams rely on | Quotes, scheduling, stock and reporting in one place, with different views per role | £40,000 upwards |

These are build costs, agreed up front for a fixed scope. They assume we're starting from a clear description of the
process, which is what the first conversation is for.

## What drives the cost

- **How many different kinds of people use it.** An app for the office is simpler than one for the office, the
  people on site and the customer.
- **What it has to connect to.** Each system (accounts, calendar, payments, text messages, your supplier's portal)
  is a piece of work. Some connect in a day. Some, usually older ones, fight back.
- **How much of the process is rules.** "A quote over £2,000 needs a second approval, unless the customer is on the
  trade list" is easy to describe and takes real care to get right. The more of those, the longer it takes.
- **Phones and photos.** If people use it on site, with a poor signal, taking photos, it needs to work offline and
  catch up later. That's doable, and it's more work than a desktop form.
- **How much exists already.** A clean spreadsheet with consistent data is a head start. Ten years of inconsistent
  data that needs cleaning before it can be imported is a cost of its own.

## The running costs

After it's built, a small app typically costs somewhere between £20 and £150 a month to host, depending on size
and what it's connected to. That's paid to the hosting provider, in your name, and it's yours. We don't rent it to
you.

Then there's upkeep. Software needs small, regular attention: security updates, the occasional change when a
connected service changes, and the improvements you'll want once people are using it. We offer this as a small
monthly retainer or as and when you need it. Either way, you own the code and can take it elsewhere.

## Keeping the first version small

The most expensive apps are the ones that tried to do everything at once. The best first version does one process,
properly, for the people who feel the pain most. It's in use within weeks, it pays for itself, and the second
version is planned from what people actually asked for rather than what anyone guessed.

So when we quote, we'll usually quote for less than you asked for, and tell you what we've left out and why.
